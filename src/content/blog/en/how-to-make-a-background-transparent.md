Here is how to make a background transparent: remove everything behind the subject, then save the image in a format that supports transparency, such as PNG. This guide explains what a transparent background is, which formats can hold one, and how to make one on any device.

## What is a transparent background?

Every pixel in an image has a color. Formats like PNG and WebP add a fourth value, called the alpha channel, that says how see-through each pixel is. A transparent background is simply an image where the pixels around the subject are fully see-through, so whatever sits behind the image shows through.

That is why a logo with a transparent background sits neatly on a colored website header, while the same logo as a JPG shows an ugly white box around it. Editors usually show transparent areas as a gray and white checkerboard so you can see them.

## Why JPG cannot be transparent

JPG has no alpha channel. When you save an image with transparent areas as JPG, those areas are filled in, usually with white. So if you want a transparent background, the final file has to be one of these:

| Format | Transparency | Best for |
| --- | --- | --- |
| PNG | Yes, with soft edges | Logos, cut-outs, stickers, slides |
| WebP | Yes, with soft edges | Websites, smaller files |
| GIF | On or off only, rough edges | Simple graphics, animation |
| JPG | No | Photos with a background |

A common mistake is to run a JPG through a [JPG to PNG converter](/jpg-to-png) and expect the white to disappear. Converting only changes the format; the background is still part of the picture. Remove it first, then save as PNG.

## Logos and product photos

**Logos.** Scanned or photographed logos, or ones exported with a white box, are the most common reason people need a transparent background. Use the highest resolution version you have, remove the background, and keep the PNG. If you need it for a website, convert it with [PNG to WebP](/png-to-webp) for a smaller file that still keeps the transparency.

**Product photos.** Shoot the product on a plain surface with soft light, then remove the background. Keep it transparent for designs and banners, or choose white for shop listings that require a white background. A strong contrast between the product and the surface gives the cleanest edges.

**Portraits.** Clear photos of one person work well. Very fine hair can come out a little soft, so a plain wall behind the person helps.

## Other ways: Paint, Preview, Photoshop and iPhone

- **Windows Paint.** Recent versions of Paint on Windows 11 have a Remove background button. Save the result as PNG.
- **Preview on Mac.** Open the image, show the Markup toolbar and use **Instant Alpha**: drag over the background color to select it and press Delete. It works best on plain, single-color backgrounds. Save as PNG when asked.
- **Photoshop.** Use the **Remove Background** quick action, then export as PNG. It gives the most control, but it is paid software.
- **iPhone and iPad.** In the Photos app, touch and hold the subject to lift it, then tap Copy or Share. It is great for quick stickers, but you get less control over the file format.

All of these work, but they take longer on photos with detailed edges, and some need software you may not have.

## Saving and using your transparent image

- **Keep the PNG as your master copy.** Every time you save as JPG, the transparency is lost.
- **Check it on a dark and a light background** to spot any leftover halo around the edges.
- **Mind the file size.** Large transparent PNGs can be several MB. Use [compress image](/compress-image) or WebP for websites.
- **Learn the formats.** If you receive WebP files, our guide on [how to convert WebP to PNG](/blog/how-to-convert-webp-to-png) explains how to open and edit them while keeping the transparency.

With a transparent PNG in hand you can drop your logo onto an invoice, put a product on any colored banner, or make stickers, without a white box in sight.
