If you are wondering how to convert HEIC to JPG on Mac, the quickest built-in way is in Finder: select your photos, right-click, choose **Quick Actions**, then **Convert Image**, and pick JPEG. Preview and the Photos app can do it too, and if you'd rather use a website, the free [ConvertLime HEIC to JPG converter](/heic-to-jpg) works in Safari or Chrome with nothing to install.

## Why convert at all if a Mac opens HEIC?

Your Mac opens HEIC photos with no trouble. The problem starts when the photo leaves your Mac: Windows PCs, many websites, job and visa upload forms, and older programs often reject HEIC. Turning the photo into a JPG means it opens everywhere. If you're curious about the format itself, read [what a HEIC file is](/blog/what-is-a-heic-file).

## Method 1: How to convert HEIC to JPG on Mac with Quick Actions

This works on macOS Monterey and newer, including Sonoma and Sequoia.

1. Open **Finder** and go to the folder with your HEIC photos.
2. Select the photos. Hold **Command** and click to pick several, or press **Command-A** to select all.
3. Right-click (or hold **Control** and click) one of the selected photos.
4. Choose **Quick Actions**, then **Convert Image**.
5. Set **Format** to **JPEG**. Choose an **Image Size** – **Actual Size** keeps the full resolution.
6. Click **Convert to JPEG**.

The new JPG files appear in the same folder, next to the HEIC originals. Nothing is deleted.

## Method 2: Use Preview's Export option

Preview gives you a quality slider, which helps if you need a smaller file.

1. Double-click the HEIC photo. It opens in **Preview**.
2. In the menu bar, choose **File**, then **Export**.
3. Next to **Format**, choose **JPEG**.
4. Move the **Quality** slider. Towards **Best** gives the sharpest picture; moving it left makes a smaller file.
5. Choose where to save it and click **Save**.

To do several at once, select them all in Finder and open them together in Preview. In the sidebar, select every thumbnail (**Command-A**), then use **File → Export Selected Images**. Click **Options** in that window to choose JPEG.

## Method 3: Export from the Photos app

If the photos synced from your iPhone into the Photos app, export them from there.

1. Open **Photos** and select the pictures you want.
2. Choose **File**, then **Export**, then **Export Photos** (the menu shows the number of photos, such as "Export 3 Photos").
3. Set **Photo Kind** to **JPEG** and choose a quality.
4. Click **Export**, pick a folder, and click **Export** again.

Be careful not to choose **Export Unmodified Original** – that one keeps the HEIC format.

## Method 4: Convert in your browser with ConvertLime

This is handy on a Mac that isn't yours, an older Mac without Quick Actions, or when you also want a ZIP file to email.

3. Wait a second for each one. The work happens on your Mac – your photos are not uploaded.

## Make the JPG small enough to upload

A JPG is usually bigger than the HEIC it came from. If a website says your photo is too large:

- Use Preview's **Quality** slider when exporting (Method 2), or
- Run the JPG through the free [image compressor](/compress-image), or
- Use [resize image to 100KB](/resize-image-to-100kb) when a form asks for a set size.

## Stop your iPhone sending HEIC to your Mac

You can't change how a Mac stores photos, but you can change what the iPhone creates. On the iPhone, go to **Settings → Camera → Formats** and choose **Most Compatible** to take JPG photos from now on. Our [iPhone HEIC to JPG guide](/blog/how-to-convert-heic-to-jpg-on-iphone) explains the trade-offs, and [HEIC vs JPEG](/blog/heic-vs-jpeg) helps you decide which format to keep.

## Troubleshooting

**Convert Image is missing from Quick Actions.** Right-click a photo, choose **Quick Actions → Customize**, and make sure **Convert Image** is ticked. If it isn't listed at all, your macOS is older than Monterey; use Preview or the browser method.

**The JPG has a different date.** The file's "created" date in Finder changes to today, but the date the photo was taken is normally kept inside the photo.

**I converted a Live Photo and lost the motion.** A JPG can only hold a still picture. The motion part stays in your Photos library.
