It works the same on Windows, Mac, iPhone and Android, and your files never leave your device. This guide covers each method, plus what happens to transparent backgrounds and how to keep the pages in the right order.

## Why turn PNG files into a PDF?

PNG is the format your computer and phone use for **screenshots**, and many scanner apps and design tools save in PNG too. That is great for sharp text and clean lines, but a pile of separate PNG files is awkward to share. A PDF fixes that:

- **One file instead of ten.** Order confirmations, chat screenshots, receipts or the pages of a scanned contract travel together.
- **Forms want PDF.** Job portals, banks, schools and visa sites often accept only PDF uploads.
- **It prints cleanly.** A PDF keeps its page size, so the printout looks like what you see on screen.
- **It opens everywhere.** Every phone, computer and email app can show a PDF without extra software.

## How to convert PNG to PDF in your browser

1. Open the [PNG to PDF converter](/png-to-pdf).
2. Drop your PNG files into the box, or click to choose them. You can also paste a screenshot straight from your clipboard with **Ctrl + V** (or **Cmd + V** on Mac).
3. Choose a page size:
   - **Same as image** – each page is exactly the size of the PNG. Best for screenshots you will read on screen, because nothing is shrunk.
   - **A4** – standard paper with a small margin. Best for scanned documents and anything you will print or upload to an official form. Big images are scaled down to fit; small ones are not stretched.
4. Click **Download PDF**. Every PNG becomes its own page in one file.

There is no sign-up, no watermark and no file limit. The PDF is built inside your browser, so even private screenshots of bank details or IDs are never sent to a server. If your files are a mix of PNG, JPG and iPhone photos, use [Image to PDF](/image-to-pdf) instead.

### Getting the page order right

Pages appear in the order you add the files. When you select many PNGs at once, the order usually follows the file names, so it helps to rename them first (for example `01-front.png`, `02-back.png`). If one page ends up in the wrong place, remove it with the **X** button and add it again: it will move to the end.

## What happens to transparency in a PDF?

PNG can have see-through areas, for example a logo or a signature on a transparent background. A PDF page is meant to look like paper, so ConvertLime puts each PNG on a **white background** before adding it. Your signature or logo will look exactly the same, just on white instead of a checkerboard.

Inside the PDF, each image is stored as a high-quality JPG (92%). That keeps the file much smaller than raw PNG data while text in screenshots stays crisp. If you need a transparent image rather than a document, keep the PNG as it is.

## How to convert PNG to PDF on Windows

Windows 10 and 11 include a virtual printer called **Microsoft Print to PDF**:

1. In **File Explorer**, select one PNG, or hold **Ctrl** and click several.
2. Right-click and choose **Print** (on Windows 11, click **Show more options** first).
3. Set the printer to **Microsoft Print to PDF** and pick a layout such as **Full page photo**.
4. Untick **Fit picture to frame** so the edges of your screenshot are not cut off.
5. Click **Print**, name the file and click **Save**.

The catch: the print dialog often crops or shrinks wide screenshots. If the text looks cut off, use the browser converter with **Same as image** instead.

## How to convert PNG to PDF on a Mac

1. Select your PNG files in Finder, right-click and choose **Open With → Preview**.
2. Drag the thumbnails in the sidebar into the order you want.
3. For a single image, choose **File → Export as PDF**. For several, choose **File → Print**, open the **PDF** menu at the bottom left and pick **Save as PDF**.

Preview is reliable, but **Print** fits every image onto a paper-sized page, which can add white borders around screenshots.

## How to convert PNG to PDF on iPhone

1. If the screenshots are in **Photos**, select them, tap **Share** and choose **Save to Files**.
2. In the **Files** app, tap **Select**, choose the images, then tap the **More** button (three dots) and **Create PDF**.

You can also select images in Photos, tap **Share → Print**, then pinch outward on the preview to open it as a PDF and share it. Both methods work, but the order can be hard to control. In Safari, the [PNG to PDF converter](/png-to-pdf) lets you pick photos in order and save the PDF to Files in one go.

## How to make the PDF smaller

A PDF of full-resolution screenshots can still be several MB, which some upload forms reject. Two quick fixes:

- Choose **A4**: very large images are scaled down to fit the page.
- Shrink the PNGs first with the [image compressor](/compress-image), then make the PDF.
