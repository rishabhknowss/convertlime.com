## Why are iPhone photos HEIC in the first place?

Since iOS 11, the iPhone camera saves photos as **HEIC** by default. HEIC (High Efficiency Image Container) holds the same detail as a JPG in roughly half the space, which is why your phone fits so many pictures. The trouble starts when you need to send those photos somewhere else. Forms, schools, landlords and insurance companies usually ask for a PDF, and many Windows PCs and web uploaders cannot even open HEIC. Our guide to [what a HEIC file is](/blog/what-is-a-heic-file) explains the format in more detail.

A PDF solves both problems at once: it opens the same way on every device, and it keeps several photos together in one file. That makes it ideal for a scanned contract, both sides of an ID card, a set of receipts or a multi-page handwritten form.

## Getting the page order right

When you combine several photos into one PDF, the order matters, especially for multi-page documents. ConvertLime puts the pages in the order you add the files. A few tips:

- **Add photos one at a time** if the order is important. Add page 1, then page 2, and so on.
- **Rename the files** before you select them (for example `01.heic`, `02.heic`) if you add them from a folder all at once.
- **Fix a mistake** by removing the photo with its **X** button and adding it again, so it moves to the end of the list.

Check the page count on the download button before you save, so you know nothing is missing.

## How to convert HEIC to PDF on iPhone

You can make a PDF on the iPhone itself using the **Files** app:

1. In **Photos**, select the pictures, tap **Share**, and choose **Save to Files**. Pick a folder, such as On My iPhone.
2. Open the **Files** app and go to that folder.
3. Tap the **More** button (three dots), tap **Select**, and choose the photos.
4. Tap the **More** button at the bottom right and choose **Create PDF**.

The PDF appears in the same folder. The pages follow the folder's sort order, so sort by name or date first if the order matters. If you prefer fewer steps, open the [HEIC to PDF converter](/heic-to-pdf) in Safari, tap the box, pick the photos straight from your library and download.

## How to convert HEIC to PDF on a Mac

Macs open HEIC natively, so **Preview** can do the job:

1. Select all the HEIC photos in Finder, right-click and choose **Open With → Preview**.
2. In the sidebar on the left, drag the thumbnails into the order you want.
3. Click **File → Print**.
4. Open the **PDF** menu at the bottom left and choose **Save as PDF**.

There is an even faster shortcut on recent versions of macOS: select the photos in Finder, right-click, and choose **Quick Actions → Create PDF**. The pages follow the order the files are sorted in Finder.

## How to convert HEIC to PDF on Windows

Windows is the hard case. Windows 10 and 11 need the **HEIF Image Extensions** and the paid **HEVC Video Extensions** from the Microsoft Store before the Photos app can show HEIC photos. Our guide on [opening HEIC files on Windows](/blog/how-to-open-heic-files-on-windows) walks through that setup. Once HEIC opens:

1. Select the photos in **File Explorer**, right-click and choose **Print** (on Windows 11, click **Show more options** first).
2. Set the printer to **Microsoft Print to PDF**.
3. Untick **Fit picture to frame** if you do not want the edges cropped.
4. Click **Print** and save the file.

If you would rather not install or pay for anything, the [HEIC to PDF converter](/heic-to-pdf) runs in Edge or Chrome and reads HEIC photos directly.

## How big will the PDF be?

HEIC is so efficient that the PDF is often larger than the photos you started with. Inside a PDF, each photo is stored as a high-quality JPEG, so a 12-megapixel iPhone photo may take 2 to 4 MB per page. Ten pages can easily pass the limit of an email or upload form.

To keep the PDF small:

- **Compress the photos first** with the [image compressor](/compress-image), then turn the smaller JPGs into a PDF with [JPG to PDF](/jpg-to-pdf). Our guide on [reducing image size without losing quality](/blog/how-to-reduce-image-size-without-losing-quality) explains good settings.
- **Split long documents** into two or three PDFs if a form has a strict size limit.
- **Crop out the background** before converting, so each page only holds what matters.

If you only need the photos to open on another device, and not a PDF, convert them with [HEIC to JPG](/heic-to-jpg) instead. And if you just want ordinary JPGs in a PDF, see our guide on [how to convert JPG to PDF](/blog/how-to-convert-jpg-to-pdf).
