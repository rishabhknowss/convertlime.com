A proforma invoice is a preliminary bill that a seller sends before a sale is completed. It shows the buyer exactly what they will get and what it will cost, including tax and shipping, so they can approve the purchase, arrange payment in advance, or apply for an import license. It looks like an invoice, but it is not a demand for payment and usually is not a tax document. ## What does proforma invoice mean?

"Pro forma" is Latin for "as a matter of form". A proforma invoice is an invoice in form only: it has the layout of a real invoice, with seller, buyer, items, prices and totals, but it describes a sale that has not happened yet. It is a good-faith estimate of the final invoice.

Because nothing has been delivered, a proforma invoice does not create a debt in the way a final invoice does, and it is usually not entered in the books as a sale.

## When is a proforma invoice used?

- **Payment in advance.** When a seller wants a deposit or full payment before shipping or starting work, the proforma tells the buyer the exact amount and how to pay.
- **International trade and customs.** Importers often need a proforma to apply for an import license, open a letter of credit, or arrange foreign currency. Customs may also accept a proforma when goods are sent before the final invoice exists, for example samples or free replacements.
- **Internal approval.** Many companies need a document with exact prices before a purchase order can be signed. A proforma gives the buying team what they need.
- **Confirming a large order.** It sets out the quantities, prices and delivery terms in writing, so both sides agree before anything ships.

## Proforma invoice vs commercial invoice vs quote

| | Quote | Proforma invoice | Commercial or final invoice |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| When | Early, while the buyer is choosing | After the buyer says yes, before delivery | After delivery or shipment |
| Purpose | Offer a price | Confirm the details and request prepayment | Request payment for a completed sale |
| Payment requested? | No | Optional, often a prepayment | Yes |
| Recorded as a sale? | No | Usually no | Yes |
| Tax document? | No | Usually no | Yes, where tax rules require it |

A quote can be loose: it may list options or ranges. A proforma invoice is more precise, because it describes the exact deal the buyer is about to accept. The commercial invoice is the final, official document, and in exports it is the one customs uses to assess duties.

## What to include on a proforma invoice

- **The title "Proforma invoice"**, clearly at the top, so nobody mistakes it for a final invoice.
- **Your details and the buyer's details**: names, addresses, contact details and tax numbers if you have them.
- **A reference number and the date**, such as PF-0001.
- **A description of each item**: quantity, unit price and line total. For exports, add weights, the country of origin and HS codes if you know them.
- **Totals**: subtotal, any discount, estimated tax, shipping and the total.
- **Terms**: payment terms, delivery terms such as Incoterms (FOB, CIF, DAP), the expected delivery date and how long the offer is valid.
- **Payment details** if you are asking for a prepayment.

## A note on tax and accounting

Because a proforma invoice describes a future sale, in most countries it does not count as a tax invoice: the buyer usually cannot use it to reclaim VAT or GST, and the seller usually does not book it as revenue. The exact rules, including how to treat prepayments, differ from country to country, so check with your accountant or tax office if you are unsure.
