If you are wondering how to convert a picture to PDF on iPhone, you have four free options and none of them needs an app: the **Print** trick in the Photos app, **Create PDF** in the Files app, sharing to **Books**, or a browser tool such as ConvertLime's [image to PDF](/image-to-pdf) in Safari. The browser tool is the quickest when you want several photos in one PDF with a choice of page size, and your photos are never uploaded. This guide walks through each method step by step.

## Which method should you use?

| Method | Best for | Several photos in one PDF? | Page size choice |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Photos app (Print) | A quick PDF from the camera roll | Yes | No, always letter or A4 |
| Files app (Create PDF) | Photos already saved in Files | Yes | No, matches the photo |
| Books app | Keeping a PDF to read later | One photo at a time | No |
| ConvertLime in Safari | Forms, applications, email | Yes, in your order | Same as image or A4 |

All four work with photos taken on the iPhone camera, screenshots and images saved from messages or websites.

## How to convert a picture to PDF on iPhone with ConvertLime

This works in Safari, Chrome or any other browser on your iPhone, and the PDF is made on the phone itself.

1. **Open the [image to PDF tool](/image-to-pdf)** in Safari.
2. **Tap the box and choose Photo Library.** Select every picture you want in the PDF, then tap Add. You can also choose Browse to pick images from Files.
3. **Check the order.** Each picture becomes one page, in the order you added them.
4. **Pick a page size.** Same as image keeps each page the size of the photo, with no white border. A4 puts each photo on a standard page, which is better for printing and for forms that expect document pages.
5. **Tap Download PDF.** Safari asks where to save it; choose Files and pick a folder, or open it and share it straight to Mail or WhatsApp.

iPhone photos are often HEIC files. ConvertLime opens HEIC directly, so there is nothing to convert first. If you only have HEIC photos, the [HEIC to PDF](/heic-to-pdf) page does the same job, and [JPG to PDF](/jpg-to-pdf) is there for JPG pictures. To learn more about the format, read [what a HEIC file is](/blog/what-is-a-heic-file).

## Use the Print trick in the Photos app

The Photos app has no Save as PDF button, but the print screen builds a PDF for you.

1. Open **Photos** and tap **Select**, then tap every picture you want in the PDF.
2. Tap the **Share** button and scroll down to **Print**.
3. On the Print Options screen, tap the **Share** button at the top. On older versions of iOS, first pinch outward on the page preview to open it full screen, then tap Share.
4. Choose **Save to Files**, pick a folder, and tap Save.

Each photo goes on its own page, sized to letter or A4 paper depending on your region. You cannot pick the page size or remove the white margins, but it is fast and uses nothing but the built-in apps.

## Use Create PDF in the Files app

If your pictures are already in the Files app, for example scans or downloads, Files can turn them into a PDF in one tap.

1. Open **Files** and go to the folder with your images.
2. For one picture, touch and hold it, then tap **Quick Actions** and **Create PDF**.
3. For several, tap the **More** button (three dots), tap **Select**, choose the images, then tap the More button at the bottom and choose **Create PDF**.

The new PDF appears in the same folder. Photos in the Photos app need to be saved to Files first (Share, then Save to Files), which is the main reason this method takes a little longer.

## Save a picture as a PDF with the Books app

The Books app quietly turns any image you share to it into a PDF.

1. Open the picture in **Photos** and tap **Share**.
2. Scroll the row of apps and tap **Books**.
3. Books opens the new PDF. To send it somewhere else, open it, tap the screen, and use the Share button.

This is handy for a single receipt or document you want to keep in your library, but it handles one picture at a time and keeps the PDF inside Books, so it is not the best choice for a multi-page file.

## Tips for a clean PDF

- **Shoot documents flat and in good light.** Lay the page on a dark surface and hold the phone straight above it so the edges are square.
- **Crop before you convert.** Use Edit in the Photos app to cut away the table and your fingers.
- **Watch the file size.** Full-size iPhone photos are 2 to 5 MB each, so a ten-page PDF can be too big for an upload form. [Compress the images](/compress-image) first if there is a limit.
- **Name the file clearly.** Rename it in Files to something like "passport-scan.pdf" before you send it.

## Is it safe to make a PDF from personal photos?

ID cards, contracts and bank letters are exactly the kind of pictures people turn into PDFs, so privacy matters. The Photos, Files and Books methods all run on your iPhone. ConvertLime also builds the PDF inside your browser: the pictures are read and placed into the PDF on your phone, and nothing is sent to a server. There is no account, no watermark and no limit on the number of photos.
