## Why websites give you AVIF files

AVIF is a newer image format built on the AV1 video codec. Its big selling point is size: an AVIF photo is often half the size of a JPG that looks the same. For a website that serves millions of images, that means faster pages and lower bandwidth bills, so shops, news sites, blogs and image CDNs now send AVIF to every browser that can show it.

The catch shows up when you save one of those pictures. Your browser displays the AVIF without trouble, but the file you save ends in `.avif`, and that is where problems start. If you want the full background on the format, read our guide to [what an AVIF file is](/blog/what-is-an-avif-file).

## Why your apps won't open AVIF

Browsers moved quickly to support AVIF. Much of the other software you use did not:

- **Older Windows photo viewers and editors** cannot read AVIF at all. Windows 11 Photos usually can, but some PCs need the free AV1 Video Extension from the Microsoft Store first.
- **Word, PowerPoint and many design tools** may reject the file or show a blank box.
- **Upload forms** for jobs, visas, exams, marketplaces and print shops usually accept only JPG or PNG.
- **Messaging apps and email clients** sometimes send AVIF as a plain attachment that the other person cannot preview.

JPG, on the other hand, opens everywhere. Converting is the simplest fix.

## How to convert AVIF to JPG on Windows

If your PC can already open AVIF files in Photos, Paint can save them as JPG:

1. Right-click the AVIF file and choose **Open with → Paint**.
2. Click **File → Save as → JPEG picture**.
3. Choose a name and a folder, then click **Save**.

If Paint or Photos cannot open the file, install the free **AV1 Video Extension** from the Microsoft Store, or skip the setup and use the browser converter in Edge or Chrome. Paint only handles one picture at a time, so for a folder full of images the browser tool is much quicker.

## How to convert AVIF to JPG on a Mac

Recent versions of macOS open AVIF in **Preview**:

1. Double-click the AVIF file to open it in Preview.
2. Click **File → Export**.
3. Set **Format** to **JPEG** and drag the quality slider towards **Best**.
4. Click **Save**.

For several files, select them all in Finder, open them together in Preview, select every thumbnail in the sidebar, then choose **File → Export Selected Images** and pick JPEG. If your Mac runs an older version of macOS and Preview cannot open the file, use the browser converter in Safari.

## How to convert AVIF to JPG on iPhone

iPhones running iOS 16 or later can display AVIF, but the Photos app has no option to save a picture as JPG. You have two simple choices:

- **Use the browser tool.** Open the [AVIF to JPG converter](/avif-to-jpg) in Safari, tap the box, choose your AVIF files from **Files** or **Photos**, and tap **Download**. The JPGs go to your Downloads folder in the Files app.
- **Use Shortcuts.** Create a shortcut with the **Convert Image** action set to JPEG, then run it from the Share sheet. This takes a few minutes to set up but works offline afterwards.

## How to keep the quality high

A few tips make sure your JPG looks as good as the AVIF:

- **Keep quality at 85–95%.** The 90% default is a good balance. Going to 100% makes the file much bigger with no visible gain.
- **Convert once, from the original.** Every time a JPG is saved again it loses a little detail, so always convert from the AVIF rather than re-saving an old JPG.
- **Expect a bigger file.** A JPG of the same picture is often two or three times the size of the AVIF. That is normal. If a form has a size limit, run the result through the [image compressor](/compress-image).
- **Choose PNG for graphics.** Logos, screenshots and images with transparency look better as PNG than as JPG.
