The quickest way to compress a photo on iPhone is to open ConvertLime's free [Compress Image](/compress-image) tool in Safari, pick the photo, move the quality slider (or type a maximum size in KB), and tap **Download**. It takes a few seconds, works without an app or account, and the photo is never sent anywhere. Below is how to do that, plus three built-in iPhone options, so you can pick what suits you.

## Why you might need a smaller photo

iPhone photos are big. That is great for keeping memories, but a problem when:

- An email bounces because the attachment is too large
- A website or form says "file must be under 1MB" or "under 200KB"
- You want to send a lot of photos without waiting
- Your phone or iCloud storage is nearly full

## 4 ways to compress a photo on iPhone

| Method | Choose exact size? | Keeps photo in Photos? | Best for |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| ConvertLime in Safari | Yes, quality and max KB | Makes a new copy | Forms, uploads, any size |
| Mail app size option | Small, Medium, Large | Sends a copy | Emailing photos |
| Crop in Photos | No | Edits the photo | Cutting out empty parts |
| Shortcuts app | Pixels only | Makes a new copy | People who like to tinker |

### 1. Compress the photo in Safari with ConvertLime

1. Open **Safari** and go to the [Compress Image](/compress-image) page.
2. Tap the box and choose **Photo Library**, then pick your photo. You can choose several at once.
3. Move the **quality** slider. Lower means a smaller file. If a website gave you a limit, type it in the **maximum size in KB** box instead.
4. Tap **Download**. Safari saves it to the **Downloads** folder in the **Files** app.
5. To put it back in Photos, open the file in Files, tap the **Share** button, and choose **Save Image**.

iPhone photos are often HEIC. The compressor accepts them and gives you a JPG, which every website and computer can open. If you only need to change the format, see [what a HEIC file is](/blog/what-is-a-heic-file).

### 2. Pick a smaller size when emailing

When you add photos to an email in the **Mail** app and the attachments are large, iPhone usually asks which size to send: **Small**, **Medium**, **Large** or **Actual Size**. Choose Small or Medium to send a lighter copy. Your original photo is not changed.

This only works for email, and you do not get to choose an exact KB size.

### 3. Crop away what you do not need

Open the photo in **Photos**, tap **Edit**, then the **crop** button, and drag the corners in. A cropped photo has fewer pixels, so the file gets smaller. You can undo this later with **Revert**.

Cropping alone rarely gets a photo under a strict limit, but it helps a lot before compressing – especially for ID or form photos where only the face matters.

### 4. Use the Shortcuts app

The free **Shortcuts** app from Apple has a **Resize Image** action that sets a new width in pixels, and a **Convert Image** action that can save as JPEG. You can build a small shortcut that resizes and saves a copy. It works, but it takes some setting up and you cannot aim for an exact file size.

## How small should your photo be?

| What you are doing | A sensible target |
| --- | --- |
| Emailing a few photos | 500KB – 1MB each |
| Uploading to a website or online shop | 200KB – 500KB |
| Exam, job or government form | Whatever the form says, often under 100KB |
| Keeping a family photo for printing | Do not compress – keep the original |

For a strict form limit, use a fixed-size tool: [Resize Image to 100KB](/resize-image-to-100kb) or [Resize Image to 200KB](/resize-image-to-200kb). They lower the quality step by step and only make the picture smaller in pixels if they really have to.

## How to check the photo's size on iPhone

In the **Photos** app, open the photo and tap the **i** button (or swipe up). Recent iOS versions show the file size, such as "2.4 MB", along with the camera details.

For a file you downloaded, open the **Files** app, press and hold the file, and tap **Get Info**. The size is listed there.

## Stop photos being so big to begin with

If you often need smaller photos, you can make new ones lighter:

1. Open **Settings** and tap **Camera**.
2. Tap **Formats**.
3. **High Efficiency** saves photos as HEIC, which is roughly half the size of a JPG. **Most Compatible** saves JPG, which is bigger but opens everywhere.

If you turn on higher-resolution options such as ProRAW or 48MP, files become much larger, so leave those off unless you need them.

## Need to send many photos as one file?

If a website asks for one document instead of several photos, put them into a PDF with the [Image to PDF](/image-to-pdf) tool. Each photo goes on its own page, and you can choose pages the same size as the photo or standard A4.
