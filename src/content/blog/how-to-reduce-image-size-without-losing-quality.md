To reduce image size without losing quality, save the photo as a JPG at a medium-high quality (around 75–85) and, if it is much larger than it needs to be, lower its width and height first. That usually makes the file several times smaller while it still looks the same on screen. The free [Compress Image](/compress-image) tool on ConvertLime does this in your browser: drop in a photo, set the quality, and compare before you download.

## Why a smaller file can look just as good

A photo straight from a phone camera holds far more detail than a screen can show. A typical phone screen or laptop shows a photo at a fraction of its real pixel size. Compressing removes detail your eyes cannot pick out at normal viewing size, so the file shrinks while the picture looks unchanged.

Quality is lost when you push too far – very low settings, or squeezing the same photo many times. The steps below help you stop at the right point.

## How to reduce image size with ConvertLime

1. Open the [Compress Image](/compress-image) tool.
2. Drop your photos into the box, or tap to choose them. JPG, PNG, WebP, AVIF and iPhone HEIC photos all work.
3. Set the **quality** slider. Start around **80**.
4. If a website gave you a limit, type it into the **maximum size in KB** box and the tool will aim to stay under it.
5. Look at the new size and the preview. If it still looks sharp, download it. If you can see blur, raise the quality a little.

The result is a JPG, which opens on every phone, computer and website.

## 5 ways to make a photo smaller and keep it sharp

### 1. Pick a sensible quality, not the lowest

| Quality setting | File size | How it looks |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 90–100 | Largest | Same as the original |
| 75–85 | Much smaller | Looks the same on screen |
| 60–70 | Small | Fine for small previews, some softness |
| Below 50 | Smallest | Blocky edges and smudged detail |

For most photos, the middle row gives the best balance.

### 2. Shrink the pixels if the photo is huge

If a photo is 4000 pixels wide but will be shown in an email or on a web page about 1200 pixels wide, those extra pixels are just weight. Making the photo smaller in pixels first gives a big drop in file size with no visible loss.

On a **Mac**, open the photo in **Preview** and choose **Tools → Adjust Size**. On **Windows**, open it in **Paint** and click **Resize**, then choose **Pixels**.

### 3. Use the right format

- **Photos:** use JPG. A photo saved as PNG can be several times bigger for no benefit. Convert it with [PNG to JPG](/png-to-jpg).
- **Screenshots, logos, text:** PNG keeps sharp edges and flat colours clean.
- **Websites:** WebP is often smaller than JPG at the same look. Try [JPG to WebP](/jpg-to-webp) if your website supports it.

### 4. Compress once, from the original

Every time a JPG is saved again at lower quality, a little more detail goes. Always start from the original camera photo, not a copy that has already been sent through WhatsApp or downloaded from social media.

### 5. Crop out what you do not need

Empty sky, floor or background still takes up space. Cropping it away makes the file smaller and puts the attention on what matters.

## What size should you aim for?

| Where the photo is going | Good target |
| --- | --- |
| Email attachment | 300KB – 1MB |
| Website or blog photo | 100KB – 300KB |
| Online shop product photo | 100KB – 300KB |
| Exam, job or government form | Whatever the form says |
| Printing at home | Keep the original |

For a fixed limit, the exact-size tools are quicker: [Resize Image to 200KB](/resize-image-to-200kb) and [Resize Image to 100KB](/resize-image-to-100kb) lower the quality step by step and only shrink the pixels if they need to. For very small form limits, see [how to resize an image to 50KB](/blog/how-to-resize-image-to-50kb).

## Common mistakes that ruin quality

- **Going straight to the lowest setting.** Try 80 first. Most of the size saving happens between 100 and 75.
- **Compressing screenshots as JPG at low quality.** Text gets fuzzy halos. Keep screenshots as PNG, or use a high quality.
- **Making a small photo bigger.** Increasing pixels never adds detail back – it just makes the file larger and softer.
- **Overwriting your original.** Keep the full-size photo and save the compressed version as a copy.
