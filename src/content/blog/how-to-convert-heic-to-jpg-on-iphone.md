To change HEIC to JPG on iPhone, open the free [ConvertLime HEIC to JPG converter](/heic-to-jpg) in Safari, pick your photos, and download the JPGs – no app needed. To make your iPhone take JPG photos from now on, go to **Settings → Camera → Formats** and choose **Most Compatible**. Both are explained below, along with a few other built-in tricks.

## First, decide: old photos or new photos?

There are two separate jobs here, and they have different fixes.

- **Photos you already took** are stored as HEIC. A setting change won't touch them. You need to convert them (Methods 1 and 2).
- **Photos you take from now on** can be saved as JPG straight away (Method 3).

| What you want | Best method | Cost |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Send a few existing photos as JPG | ConvertLime in Safari | Free |
| Save a JPG copy in the Files app | Copy and paste into Files | Free |
| Take JPG photos from now on | Camera → Formats → Most Compatible | Free, but uses more storage |
| Get JPGs on a PC by cable | Photos → Transfer to Mac or PC → Automatic | Free |

## Method 1: Convert HEIC to JPG on iPhone in Safari

This works on any iPhone with iOS 17 or 18 and handles many photos at once.

1. Open **Safari** and go to the [HEIC to JPG converter](/heic-to-jpg).
2. Tap the box. A menu appears – choose **Photo Library** to pick from your photos, or **Choose File** to pick from the Files app.
3. Select one or more photos and tap **Add** (or **Done**).
4. Each photo converts in a second or two, right on your phone.
5. Tap **Download** for each photo, or **Download all as ZIP**.
6. Open the **Files** app and look in **Downloads** to find them. Tap a ZIP file once to unpack it.

From the Files app you can attach the JPGs to an email, upload them to a website, or share them to WhatsApp.

Need the photos in a single document instead? [HEIC to PDF](/heic-to-pdf) does that – handy for receipts, ID scans or forms.

## Method 2: Copy and paste into the Files app

This uses only Apple's own apps. On recent iOS versions, a photo pasted into Files is usually saved as a JPG.

1. Open **Photos** and tap **Select**, then pick your photos.
2. Tap the **Share** button and choose **Copy Photos** (or **Copy Photo**).
3. Open the **Files** app and go to **On My iPhone** (or any folder).
4. Press and hold on an empty part of the screen and tap **Paste**.
5. Check the file names – they should now end in **.jpg** or **.jpeg**.

If they still end in .heic, use Method 1 instead.

## Method 3: Make your iPhone take JPG photos

1. Open **Settings**.
2. Tap **Camera**, then **Formats**.
3. Choose **Most Compatible**.

From now on, the Camera saves JPG photos. The trade-off is space: a JPG often takes up close to twice the room of a HEIC photo that looks the same. If your iPhone is often nearly full, you may prefer to keep **High Efficiency** and convert only when you need to. Our [HEIC vs JPEG comparison](/blog/heic-vs-jpeg) goes through this choice.

## Method 4: Let the iPhone convert when you share

The iPhone already converts many photos to JPG when they leave your phone:

- **Mail and most messaging apps** usually receive a JPG.
- **Cable to a computer:** go to **Settings → Photos**, scroll to **Transfer to Mac or PC**, and choose **Automatic**. Photos copied to a PC then arrive as JPG.
- **AirDrop to a Mac** and **iCloud links** often keep the HEIC, because Apple devices can open it.

If someone on Windows still receives a HEIC file, send them the [HEIC to JPG converter](/heic-to-jpg) link – or point them to our guide on [converting HEIC to JPG on Windows](/blog/how-to-convert-heic-to-jpg-on-windows).

## Photo too big to upload?

JPGs are larger than HEIC photos, and some forms set strict limits. After converting:

- Use the [image compressor](/compress-image) to shrink the file while keeping it sharp.
- Use [resize image to 100KB](/resize-image-to-100kb) or [resize image to 50KB](/resize-image-to-50kb) when a form asks for a set size.

Both tools work in Safari on your iPhone, the same way as the converter.

## Quick answers to common hiccups

**Safari says the download finished but I can't find it.** Open the **Files** app, tap **Browse**, and look in **Downloads** (it may be under **iCloud Drive** or **On My iPhone**).

**The photo lost its Live Photo motion.** A JPG holds one still picture. The moving version stays safe in your Photos app.

**I changed the setting but old photos are still HEIC.** That's expected. The setting only affects new photos – convert older ones with Method 1.
